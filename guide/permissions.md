## Creating a schedule
- The **owner** of a schedule is the person who creates it. Issues are created **as the owner**, so the owner is the reporter.
- The owner needs the **Create issues** permission in the target project. Saving is refused otherwise.
- An administrator chooses who may create schedules (see [Administration](admin.html)):
  - **Project administrators** (default), or
  - **Everyone with "Create issues"** in the project.
- There is no group option.

## Changing a schedule
Edit, pause, resume and delete: the owner or a Jira administrator.

## What you see
You see the schedules of projects you can browse. Jira administrators see what their own project access allows in the lists, and everything in the administration page's error list and log.

## Reassigning the owner
If the owner leaves, is deactivated, or loses access, issue creation fails (see [Errors](errors.html)). A Jira administrator opens **Administration, Schedules in error, Reassign owner**, searches a user, and picks one. Only users who can create issues in that project are offered. New issues are created in the new owner's name.

## Permissions RepeatWise asks Jira for
Read and write Jira work (to create, comment on, transition and search issues as the owner), read users (for the owner picker, run as the admin), and read projects. Jira Software sprint read and write, and board read, are used to add new issues to a sprint. Jira Service Management request read and write are used to list request types and raise [service requests](service-requests.html) as the owner. The manifest has no external scope. See [Data and privacy](privacy.html).

## After an app update
When an update adds permissions, each user may be asked to **Allow access** once. The schedule owner must do this for their schedules to keep running. Until then, issues cannot be created as the owner and the schedule shows an error (see [Errors](errors.html)).
